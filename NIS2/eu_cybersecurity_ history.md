# The Evolution of EU Cybersecurity (2004 - 2020)

This document traces the history, major accomplishments, and key agencies of the EU's cybersecurity journey leading up to the Digital Decade.

---

## 1. Why Cybersecurity Matters to the EU

*   **Economic Competitiveness:** Safe environments build the digital trust required to fuel innovation and economic growth.
*   **Privacy:** Robust security measures are vital to protect personal data and prevent massive breaches.
*   **Defense:** Critical for countering state-sponsored attacks, cybercrime, and cyber-espionage.
*   **Citizen Trust:** Empowering and protecting individuals from online fraud, risks, and abuse.

---

## 2. Historical Cyber Timeline (2004 - 2019)

[2004: ENISA Established] ──> [2013: First Cyber Strategy] ──> [2016: NIS1 Adopted] ──> [2019: EU Cybersecurity Act]
### 2004 | ENISA (EU Agency for Cybersecurity)
*   **Headquarters:** Athens, Greece.
*   **Role:** An independent EU agency acting as a center of expertise. It provides advice, technical guidelines, and fosters cooperation between Member States.

### 2013 | First Comprehensive Cybersecurity Strategy
*   **Focus:** Building a secure and trustworthy digital environment.
*   **Legacy Accomplishments:** Directly paved the way for the creation of **GDPR** (General Data Protection Regulation) and the **EC3** (European Cybercrime Centre).
*   **The 5 Original Priorities:**
    1.  **Cyber Resilience:** Formed **CERT-EU** and promoted Public-Private Partnerships (PPPs).
    2.  **Cybercrime Reduction:** Strengthened legal frameworks and law enforcement capabilities.
    3.  **Cyber Defense:** Created a common defense policy, coordinated cyber exercises, and enhanced info-sharing.
    4.  **Industrial/Tech Resources:** Promoted security standardization and product certifications.
    5.  **International Cooperation:** Fostered partnerships globally to support an open, stable cyberspace.

### 2016 | NIS Directive (NIS1)
*   The first EU-wide law on cybersecurity. It forced Member States to build national capabilities and established security/incident reporting rules.

### 2019 | EU Cybersecurity Act
*   **The 3 Primary Objectives:**
    1.  Coordinate EU-wide cybersecurity responses.
    2.  Create a unified, voluntary **Cybersecurity Certification Framework** for products, services, and processes (supported by the **ECCG** - European Cybersecurity Certification Group).
    3.  Reinforce **ENISA** by granting it a **permanent mandate** and stronger authority.
*   **Operational Boost:** Encouraged countries to establish national CSIRTs (Computer Security Incident Response Teams).

## 3. The Digital Europe Program (DIGITAL)

A strategic funding program with a budget of **€7.5 Billion** to build EU technological sovereignty.

### 5 Capacity Focus Areas:
1.  **Supercomputing**
2.  **Artificial Intelligence (AI)**
3.  **Cybersecurity & Trust**
4.  **Advanced Digital Skills**
5.  **Digital Innovation Hubs**

---

## 4. The Digital Decade (Targets by 2030)

Europe’s plan to achieve digital sovereignty by setting its own standards instead of following foreign ones (e.g., the US).

*   **Skills:** 20 Million active ICT specialists + 80% of the population possessing basic digital skills.
*   **Businesses:** Over 90% of SMBs digitized + 75% of companies adopting Cloud/AI/Big Data + doubling the number of EU Unicorn startups.
*   **Infrastructure:** Gigabit connectivity for everyone + doubling EU's share in global semiconductor production + developing the first computer with quantum acceleration.
*   **Public Services:** 100% online key public services + 100% online access to medical records (e-Health) + 80% of citizens using digital IDs.
*   **Digital Rights:** Ensuring freedom of choice online, safety, inclusion, and human-centered technology.

---

## 5. EU Cybersecurity Strategy for the Digital Decade (Dec 2020)

A major revision of the 2013 strategy to integrate security into EU digital investments (AI, Encryption, Quantum).

### 3 Action Areas:
1.  Resilience, technological sovereignty, and leadership.
2.  Operational capacity to prevent, deter, and respond.
3.  Advancing a global, open cyberspace.

### Key Outcomes:
*   Created the **Joint Cyber Unit** to respond to large-scale, cross-border incidents.
*   Strengthened the legal framework, initiating the **birth of NIS2**.
