## 1. The Legacy: NIS1 Scope & Rules (2016)

The original NIS Directive applied strictly to two limited groups of organizations:

1.  **OES (Operators of Essential Services):** Water, transport, energy, healthcare, and digital infrastructure.
2.  **RDSPs (Relevant Digital Service Providers):** Search engines, online marketplaces, and cloud computing providers (excluding small/micro enterprises).

### The 6 Core Pillars of NIS1:
*   Security Requirements
*   Risk Management
*   Incident Reporting
*   Cooperation & Info Sharing
*   Cross-border Collaboration
*   National Strategies & Certifications

---

## 2. Why Upgrade? The Core Shortcomings of NIS1

Despite its early success, NIS1 suffered from structural flaws that became evident over time:

*   **Uneven National Implementation:** Different Member States applied requirements and oversight differently. This fragmented landscape heavily burdened companies operating across borders.
*   **Exclusion of Supply Chains:** The original scope failed to address the security of third-party vendors and subcontractors.
*   **Inadequate Incident Timelines:** The reporting duties lacked the speed needed to isolate active threats.

---

## 3. Case Study: The Kaseya Ransomware Attack (July 2021)

This real-world attack proved the danger of supply chain vulnerabilities and why NIS1 was no longer sufficient.

*   **The Perpetrator:** The Russian-linked ransomware-on-demand group **REvil**.
*   **The Exploit:** Hackers exploited a zero-day vulnerability in **Kaseya VSA** (software used by Managed Service Providers to monitor client infrastructure).
*   **The Impact:** The exploit allowed attackers to deploy ransomware onto MSP networks, spreading downstream to encrypt the systems of over **1,500 organizations** globally.
*   **The Ransom:** Attackers demanded a collective ransom of **$70 Million** in Bitcoin.
*   **The Lesson:** Organizations are only as secure as their least secure third-party vendor. Securing the supply chain is a critical national security priority.

---

## 4. The Solution: Key Improvements in NIS2

The NIS2 directive addresses legacy weaknesses with stronger requirements and severe penalties for non-compliance.

| Feature | NIS1 (Old) | NIS2 (New) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Supply Chain Security** | Not covered. | **Mandatory** security risk management of all suppliers. |
| **Entity Classification** | Operators of Essential Services (OES). | Streamlined into **"Essential"** and **"Important"** categories. |
| **Reporting Window** | Loose and undefined. | Strict **72-hour reporting** requirement for significant incidents. |
| **Security Audits** | Not systematic. | Mandatory regular security audits for all covered entities. |
| **Maximum Fines** | Inconsistent across states. | Up to **2% of global annual turnover** depending on classification. |
| **Executive Liability** | Weak/Unenforced. | Authorities can issue an **injunction to bypass management** to enforce security orders. |

> 💰 **Financial Support:**
> The **Digital Europe Program** acts as the financial engine for NIS2, providing funding for businesses to upgrade their infrastructures to meet these strict requirements.

---

## 5. Scope Expansion: NIS vs. NIS2

To achieve greater harmonization, NIS2 drastically expands the sectors and entities under its jurisdiction.

[NIS1 Scope: 7 Sectors | 12 Subsectors | 33 Entity Types] 🔽 (Massive Expansion) [NIS2 Scope: 18 Sectors | 30 Subsectors | 72 Entity Types]
### Newly Added Sectors under NIS2:
*   Postal & Courier Services
*   Waste Management
*   Chemical Production & Distribution
*   Agri-food (Food Production & Processing)

### Member State Flexibility:
While NIS2 harmonizes EU security, individual **Member States retain the flexibility to expand the scope further** (for example, choosing to include local authorities under their national laws).
