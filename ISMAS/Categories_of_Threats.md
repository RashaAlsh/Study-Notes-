# Summary: 12 Categories of Threats to Information Security


---

## 1. Compromises to Intellectual Property
*   **Summary:** The unauthorized appropriation, use, duplication, or distribution of protected intellectual property (IP) such as trade secrets, copyrights, trademarks, and patents.
*   **Examples:** Software piracy, copyright infringement, or corporate data leaks of proprietary code.

## 2. Deviations in Quality of Service
*   **Summary:** Disruptions caused by an organization’s service providers failing to deliver the expected or contracted level of operational support.
*   **Examples:** Power grid outages, internet service provider (ISP) blackouts, or critical supply chain delays.

## 3. Espionage or Trespass
*   **Summary:** Unauthorized individuals gaining access to an organization’s physical or electronic premises to monitor activities, steal credentials, or harvest sensitive data.
*   **Examples:** Network hacking, unauthorized tailgating into data centers, or social engineering attacks.

## 4. Forces of Nature
*   **Summary:** Unpredictable, uncontrollable environmental and natural occurrences that cause physical damage to infrastructure or disrupt standard business operations.
*   **Examples:** Floods, earthquakes, hurricanes, fires, or lightning strikes.

## 5. Human Error or Failure
*   **Summary:** Accidental, unintended actions taken by internal personnel without malicious intent, usually due to a lack of training, awareness, or simple oversight.
*   **Examples:** Accidental file deletion, firewall misconfigurations, or clicking on phishing links.

## 6. Information Extortion
*   **Summary:** An attacker stealing or restricting access to an organization's internal data and demanding financial payment in exchange for its return or to prevent public exposure.
*   **Examples:** Ransomware deployment or corporate blackmail schemes.

## 7. Sabotage or Vandalism
*   **Summary:** Deliberate, malicious destruction, alteration, or defacement of an organization's systems, website, physical property, or reputation.
*   **Examples:** Website defacement by hacktivists, cyberterrorism, or a disgruntled employee destroying physical server racks.

## 8. Software Attacks
*   **Summary:** Malicious software (malware) or code explicitly engineered by bad actors to damage, disrupt, or gain unauthorized administrative access to computer networks and systems.
*   **Examples:** Viruses, worms, Trojan horses, logic bombs, or Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS) attacks.

## 9. Technical Hardware Failures or Errors
*   **Summary:** Physical equipment malfunctions or internal hardware flaws that cause hardware to fail entirely or behave unpredictably.
*   **Examples:** Server hard drive crashes, power supply failures, or manufacturing defects in storage arrays.

## 10. Technical Software Failures or Errors
*   **Summary:** Logic flaws, coding errors, or bugs hidden within software applications or operating systems that cause system instability or present exploitable security vulnerabilities.
*   **Examples:** Buffer overflows, zero-day vulnerabilities, or critical software application crashes.

## 11. Technological Obsolescence
*   **Summary:** Outdated or legacy infrastructure, software, or hardware that is no longer maintained or supported by the vendor, rendering it insecure.
*   **Examples:** Running legacy operating systems (like Windows XP) that no longer receive official security patches.

## 12. Theft
*   **Summary:** The illegal confiscation, appropriation, or physical removal of an organization's digital or physical assets.
*   **Examples:** Physical theft of laptops or backup drives, or digital exfiltration of customer records and financial data.
