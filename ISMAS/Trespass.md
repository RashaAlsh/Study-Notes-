# Espionage or Trespass: An Overview

## Core Definition

**Espionage or Trespass** involves unauthorized individuals or entities intentionally gaining illicit access to an organization's physical facilities, internal electronic systems, or sensitive networks. The primary motivation is to monitor organizational activities, compromise credentials, or harvest proprietary data.

### Context in Strategy

Referencing *The Art of War* concepts, this threat category represents an adversary attempting to **"know you"** without your consent. Attackers exploit internal blind spots, weaknesses, and information gaps to gather intelligence and compromise organizational security.

---

# Common Methods and Tactics

To execute espionage or trespass, threat actors typically combine technical intrusion techniques with human-focused manipulation.

## 1. Electronic Trespass (Hacking)

Attackers exploit vulnerabilities in software, unpatched systems, or misconfigured security controls to breach a secure network perimeter.

Once inside, they may:

- Install backdoors for persistent access
- Deploy packet sniffers to monitor network traffic
- Harvest sensitive records and credentials
- Conduct surveillance without detection

---

## 2. Social Engineering

Social engineering targets people rather than technology.

Common techniques include:

- Phishing emails designed to steal usernames and passwords
- Impersonating IT support personnel
- Convincing employees to bypass security procedures
- Manipulating staff into revealing access credentials or confidential information

The goal is to exploit trust and human behavior to gain unauthorized access.

---

## 3. Physical Trespass and Tailgating

Physical trespass involves bypassing physical security measures to enter restricted areas.

### Tailgating

A common tactic where an unauthorized individual closely follows an authorized employee through a secure entrance without presenting valid credentials.

Potential targets include:

- Office buildings
- Server rooms
- Data centers
- Research facilities

---

## 4. Shoulder Surfing and Dumpster Diving

### Shoulder Surfing

An attacker observes employees entering passwords or viewing sensitive information on screens.

### Dumpster Diving

Attackers search discarded materials for valuable information, such as:

- Passwords
- Printed source code
- Confidential reports
- Internal memos
- Customer records

Improper disposal of sensitive documents can provide attackers with valuable intelligence.

---

# Interconnection with Other Threats

Espionage or Trespass rarely occurs in isolation.

Attackers frequently use it as an **initial reconnaissance phase** to gather intelligence before launching additional attacks.

A common attack progression may include:

1. **Espionage or Trespass** → Gather information and identify vulnerabilities.
2. **Software Attack** → Deploy malware, ransomware, or other malicious tools.
3. **Theft or Information Extortion** → Steal sensitive data, disrupt operations, or demand payment.

This progression demonstrates how espionage serves as a foundation for more damaging cyber and physical security incidents.

---

# Key Takeaway

Espionage or Trespass focuses on obtaining unauthorized access to an organization's people, facilities, systems, or information. Whether through hacking, social engineering, physical intrusion, or information gathering, attackers use these techniques to collect intelligence and create opportunities for more severe attacks such as data theft, malware deployment, and extortion.
