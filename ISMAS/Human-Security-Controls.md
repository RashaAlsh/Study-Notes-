ISO/IEC 27001 Controls – Human Security Controls

Screening, Awareness, Confidentiality, and Event Reporting

Overview

Human factors are a critical part of information security. ISO/IEC 27001 includes controls that ensure employees are trustworthy, understand their responsibilities, protect sensitive information, and report security issues.

Key controls include:

* 6.1 Screening
* 6.3 Awareness
* 6.6 Confidentiality and Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs)
* 6.8 Information Security Event Reporting

Together, these controls strengthen the human side of an Information Security Management System (ISMS).

⸻

6.1 Screening

Overview

Organizations should perform background checks before allowing individuals to access company systems or sensitive information.

The purpose of screening is to verify that employees and contractors are suitable for their roles.

⸻

Types of Checks

Screening may include:

* Education verification
* Employment history
* Criminal background checks
* Financial background checks
* Security clearance verification

⸻

Factors Affecting Screening Requirements

The level of screening depends on:

* Local laws and regulations
* Industry requirements
* Role sensitivity
* Level of access to information

Example

Financial institutions may require stricter checks due to access to sensitive financial data.

⸻

Key Requirement

Employees should only begin working with sensitive information after required screening checks have been completed.

⸻

6.3 Awareness

Overview

All employees must understand their information security responsibilities and how their actions affect the organization’s security.

Security awareness ensures that employees contribute to protecting information assets.

⸻

Awareness Methods

Organizations can provide awareness through:

* Classroom training
* Online courses
* Emails
* Security newsletters
* Posters and reminders
* Simulated exercises

⸻

Training Topics

Security awareness training should cover:

Information Security Management System (ISMS)

* Purpose of the ISMS
* Importance of information protection

Policies and Compliance

* Organizational security policies
* Legal and regulatory requirements

Employee Responsibilities

* Protecting sensitive information
* Following security procedures
* Reporting suspicious activity

Security Procedures

Examples:

* Authentication practices
* Password protection
* Access control
* Incident reporting

⸻

Continuous Awareness

Awareness should not be a one-time activity.

Organizations should:

* Provide regular training
* Update employees on new threats
* Test understanding through assessments or exercises

⸻

6.6 Confidentiality and Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs)

Overview

Employees and third parties must protect confidential company and customer information.

Confidentiality requirements are often formalized through Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs).

⸻

NDA Contents

An NDA should define:

Confidential Information

What information must be protected.

Examples:

* Customer records
* Business plans
* Technical information
* Financial information

Confidentiality Duration

How long information must remain protected.

Allowed Use

Defines how information may be used and for what purposes.

Access Rules

Defines who is permitted to access confidential information.

Breach Actions

Defines consequences and required actions if confidentiality is violated.

⸻

Purpose

NDAs help:

* Reduce unauthorized disclosure
* Establish legal protection
* Clarify employee responsibilities

⸻

6.8 Information Security Event Reporting

Overview

Employees must report suspicious activities or security events as soon as possible.

Early reporting helps organizations identify and respond to potential incidents.

⸻

Examples of Security Events

Employees should report:

* Open doors or unauthorized access
* Broken windows or physical security issues
* Suspicious emails or phishing attempts
* Unexpected system access problems
* Lost devices
* Unusual system behavior

⸻

Event vs Incident

A reported event is reviewed to determine whether it is an actual security incident.

Example:

Security Event → Investigation → Confirmed Incident → Incident Response

Not every event becomes an incident, but every incident starts as an event.

⸻

Importance in Information Security

These controls support:

Security Goal	How These Controls Help
Confidentiality	Protect sensitive information from unauthorized disclosure
Integrity	Reduce misuse and unauthorized changes
Availability	Enable early detection and response to issues

⸻

Summary

ISO/IEC 27001 human security controls ensure that employees and contractors support information protection.

* 6.1 Screening ensures individuals are suitable before receiving access.
* 6.3 Awareness ensures employees understand security responsibilities.
* 6.6 Confidentiality & NDAs legally protect sensitive information.
* 6.8 Event Reporting ensures suspicious activities are identified and investigated early.

Together, these controls reduce human-related risks and strengthen the overall effectiveness of the ISMS.
