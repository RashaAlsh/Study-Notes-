ISO 27001 Risk Assessment Process

1. Identify Assets

The risk assessment process begins by identifying assets that need protection, including:

* People
* Systems and applications
* Networks and infrastructure
* Information and data

Organizations should understand how each asset stores, accesses, processes, or modifies information.

2. Identify Risks

Potential threats and vulnerabilities that could affect assets must be identified. Examples include:

* Cyberattacks
* Human error
* Insider threats
* System failures
* Natural disasters

Risks should be assessed based on their potential impact on the following security principles:

* Confidentiality – Preventing unauthorized disclosure of information.
* Integrity – Preventing unauthorized modification of information.
* Availability – Ensuring information remains accessible when needed.

3. Define Risk Criteria

Organizations must establish:

* A consistent risk assessment methodology
* Risk evaluation criteria
* Risk appetite (the level of risk the organization is willing to accept)

This ensures assessments are repeatable, objective, and reliable.

4. Analyze Risks

For each identified risk:

1. Assess the potential impact if the risk occurs.
2. Assess the likelihood of occurrence.
3. Calculate the overall risk level.

Risk Formula

Risk Level = Likelihood × Impact

5. Evaluate Risks

Compare calculated risk levels against the organization’s risk appetite to determine whether risks are:

* Acceptable
* Requiring treatment
* Requiring further review

6. Treat Risks

Organizations generally choose one of three treatment options:

Accept the Risk

* Used when the risk falls within the organization’s acceptable risk threshold.

Reduce/Treat the Risk

* Implement controls to lower the likelihood or impact of the risk.

Examples:

* Security policies
* Access controls
* Firewalls
* Monitoring systems
* Software updates and patching

Transfer the Risk

* Shift responsibility to another party.

Examples:

* Cyber insurance
* Outsourcing services
* Contractual agreements

Risk Ownership

Each identified risk should have a designated risk owner responsible for ensuring appropriate mitigation and monitoring.

7. Prioritize Risks

Risks should be ranked according to their severity so that the most critical risks are addressed first.

Supporting Practices

Business Impact Analysis (BIA)

A Business Impact Analysis helps organizations:

* Understand how risks affect business objectives
* Determine impact ratings
* Prioritize protection and recovery efforts

Establishing a Risk Baseline

Organizations should recognize that some level of risk will always exist.

* Baseline risks are expected.
* Only risks exceeding the organization’s risk appetite typically require treatment.

Collaborative Risk Assessment

Risk discussions should involve stakeholders from different departments and backgrounds to:

* Improve accuracy
* Reduce bias
* Encourage open communication
* Promote accountability without assigning blame

Security Controls

Risk treatment commonly results in implementing controls such as:

* Policies and procedures
* Access management
* Firewalls
* Endpoint protection
* Security monitoring
* Backup and recovery solutions
* Patch management

⸻

Key ISO 27001 Principles

* Risk management is a continuous process, not a one-time activity.
* Risk assessments should be reviewed and updated regularly.
* Residual risk will always remain after controls are implemented.
* Stakeholders should be informed of and formally accept residual risks when appropriate.
* The ISO 27001 risk management approach aligns with the principles defined in ISO 31000.

⸻

Summary

ISO 27001 requires organizations to systematically identify assets, assess risks to the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information, assign ownership, evaluate risks against risk appetite, implement appropriate treatments, and continuously monitor residual risks to support effective information security management.
