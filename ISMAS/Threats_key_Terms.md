## 1. Core Vocabulary & Definitions

The text defines several foundational terms crucial to understanding how security breaches occur:

*   **Threat:** Any event or circumstance that has the potential to adversely affect operations and assets. (The text notes that *threat source* and *threat* are used interchangeably to simplify discussion).
*   **Threat Agent:** The specific instance or component of a threat.
*   **Vulnerability:** A potential weakness in an asset or its defensive control system(s).
*   **Exploit:** A technique used to compromise a system (can be a verb or a noun). Threat agents attempt to exploit an asset illegally for personal gain.
*   **Attack / Threat Event:** An intentional or unintentional ongoing act that can damage or otherwise compromise information and the systems that support it.
*   **Loss:** A single instance of an information asset suffering damage, destruction, unintended/unauthorized modification or disclosure, or denial of use.

## 2. Strategic Principles: The Art of War in Cybersecurity

The text references the Chinese general Sun Tzu Wu’s *The Art of War* (circa 500 BC) to ground information security management in classical strategy. To adequately protect organizational assets, management must execute two fundamental imperatives:

1.  **Know Yourself:** Be thoroughly familiar with internal information assets, their inherent flaws, vulnerabilities, and the mechanisms and methods used to store, transport, process, and protect them.
2.  **Know the Threats You Face:** Be completely informed about external and internal threats to the organization's people, applications, data, and systems.

According to the text, failing to know both yourself and the enemy puts you "in danger in every battle."

---

## 3. How the Concepts Interconnect

*   **Threats vs. Attacks:** A *threat* represents a *potential* risk and is always present. An *attack* (or threat event) is an *ongoing act* that only exists when a specific action is taken.
*   **The Chain of Compromise:** **Threat agents** cause damage or steal an organization's assets by using **exploits** to take advantage of a **vulnerability** where defensive controls are missing or no longer effective. This active process leads to a **loss**.
