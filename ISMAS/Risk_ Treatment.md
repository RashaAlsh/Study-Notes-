Information Security Risk Treatment (ISO/IEC 27001 – Clause 6.1.3)

Overview

Risk treatment is the process of determining how identified information security risks will be managed and implementing appropriate controls to reduce those risks to an acceptable level.

The goal is to ensure that risks are handled consistently, documented properly, and approved by the appropriate stakeholders.

⸻

Risk Treatment Options

Organizations must decide how each identified risk will be addressed.

1. Avoid the Risk

Eliminate the activity or condition that creates the risk.

Examples:

* Discontinuing a vulnerable service
* Removing unnecessary systems
* Avoiding the storage of sensitive information

2. Accept the Risk

Take no additional action when the risk falls within the organization’s risk appetite.

Examples:

* Low-impact risks
* Risks where treatment costs exceed potential losses

3. Treat the Risk

Implement controls to reduce either the likelihood or impact of the risk.

Examples:

* Firewalls
* Multi-factor authentication (MFA)
* Security awareness training
* Access controls
* Patch management

4. Transfer the Risk

Shift responsibility for the risk to another party.

Examples:

* Cyber insurance
* Outsourcing services
* Contractual agreements with suppliers

⸻

Selecting Controls

When treating risks, organizations must select appropriate controls.

Important considerations:

* Multiple controls may be required for a single risk.
* Controls can be technical, physical, administrative, or procedural.
* A combination of technology and user awareness measures is often most effective.

Example:

To reduce phishing risks:

* Email filtering (technical control)
* Security awareness training (administrative control)

⸻

Comparing Controls with Annex A

ISO/IEC 27001 requires organizations to compare their selected controls against Annex A.

Purpose

* Ensure important controls have not been overlooked.
* Provide a recognized framework for security control selection.
* Demonstrate due diligence during audits.

Annex A Controls

Annex A contains 93 information security controls organized into four categories:

1. Organizational Controls
2. People Controls
3. Physical Controls
4. Technological Controls

Note: Annex A controls are not mandatory. Organizations should select controls based on their specific risks and business requirements.

⸻

Statement of Applicability (SoA)

The Statement of Applicability (SoA) is one of the most important documents in an Information Security Management System (ISMS).

Purpose

The SoA documents:

* Which controls have been selected
* Why controls were selected
* Why controls were excluded
* Whether controls have been implemented

Typical Contents

Item	Description
Control Reference	Annex A control identifier
Control Description	Summary of the control
Applicable?	Yes/No
Justification	Reason for inclusion or exclusion
Implementation Status	Implemented, Planned, or Not Implemented

Internal vs External Versions

Organizations may maintain:

* Internal SoA: Detailed implementation information
* External SoA: Simplified version for auditors, customers, or regulators

⸻

Risk Treatment Plan

A Risk Treatment Plan documents how selected controls will be implemented.

Typical elements include:

* Risk being addressed
* Selected controls
* Responsible owner
* Required resources
* Target implementation dates
* Success criteria

The plan serves as a roadmap for reducing identified risks.

⸻

Approval by Risk Owners

Risk owners must review and approve:

* The selected treatment plan
* Any remaining residual risks

This ensures accountability and confirms that management understands the remaining exposure after controls are implemented.

⸻

Residual Risk

Residual risk is the level of risk that remains after controls have been applied.

Key points:

* Residual risk can never be completely eliminated.
* Organizations must determine whether residual risk is acceptable.
* Risk owners should formally acknowledge and accept residual risks where appropriate.

⸻

Documentation Requirements

ISO 27001 requires organizations to maintain documented information related to risk treatment.

Documentation supports:

* Audit evidence
* Accountability
* Traceability
* Continuous improvement

Important records include:

* Risk assessments
* Risk treatment decisions
* Risk treatment plans
* Statements of Applicability
* Risk owner approvals

⸻

Summary

Risk treatment in ISO/IEC 27001 involves selecting an appropriate response to identified risks, choosing and implementing suitable controls, comparing those controls against Annex A, documenting decisions in the Statement of Applicability (SoA), obtaining approval from risk owners, and maintaining records that demonstrate accountability and compliance. The objective is to reduce risks to an acceptable level while ensuring transparency and continual improvement within the ISMS.
