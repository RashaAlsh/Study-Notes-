EU AI Act: Timeline and Implementation

Overview

The EU AI Act is being implemented gradually over several years, with different requirements taking effect at different times depending on the AI system, its risk category, and its intended use.

This phased approach gives organizations time to prepare while prioritizing the regulation of potentially harmful AI practices.

Implementation Timeline

2025: First Rules Take Effect

The first requirements began applying in February 2025, including:

* Prohibited AI practices: Certain harmful uses of AI, such as social scoring and specific manipulative practices, are banned.
* AI literacy: Organizations must take measures to ensure an adequate level of AI literacy among relevant staff and other people operating AI systems on their behalf.

Mid-2025: General-Purpose AI (GPAI) Requirements

From August 2025, obligations began applying to providers of general-purpose AI models, including large language models.

Key requirements include:

* Preparing technical documentation.
* Implementing policies to comply with EU copyright law.
* Publishing summaries of training content.
* Meeting additional requirements for models classified as having systemic risk.

These obligations primarily concern model providers, rather than ordinary end users.

August 2026: Transparency and Enforcement

The Act’s general application date is 2 August 2026, with certain exceptions and transitional arrangements.

Relevant requirements include:

* Transparency obligations for certain AI interactions and AI-generated content.
* Disclosure requirements for certain AI-generated or manipulated content, including deepfakes.
* Rules for identifying AI-generated content where applicable.
* Broader enforcement and market-surveillance arrangements.

Specific transparency obligations depend on the relevant provision and type of AI system.

December 2026: Additional Prohibitions

Further restrictions may apply under specific provisions and related implementation measures. Organizations should verify the applicable legal text and implementation dates rather than assume that all additional prohibitions begin in December 2026.

2027: High-Risk AI Systems Under Annex III

High-risk AI systems in areas listed in Annex III are generally scheduled to become subject to the relevant requirements from 2 August 2026, rather than 2027.

However, the EU has considered changes to the implementation timetable. Organizations should check the latest enacted legislation to confirm whether any amendments affect their compliance deadlines.

Examples of high-risk application areas include:

* Employment and recruitment.
* Education and vocational training.
* Access to essential services.
* Certain law-enforcement applications.
* Migration, asylum, and border control.

Depending on the system and applicable rules, requirements can include risk management, documentation, human oversight, data governance, and monitoring.

2028: AI Embedded in Regulated Products

The original AI Act timetable provides for certain requirements relating to high-risk AI systems covered by Article 6(1), associated with regulated products under Annex I, to apply from 2 August 2027, rather than 2028.

These systems may include AI used in:

* Vehicles.
* Machinery.
* Medical devices.
* Other regulated products requiring third-party conformity assessment.

Because these systems interact with existing product-safety legislation, their compliance obligations can be more complex.

Timeline at a Glance

Period	Main Focus
February 2025	Prohibited AI practices and AI literacy obligations
August 2025	General-purpose AI model requirements
August 2026	General application, transparency, and enforcement provisions, subject to exceptions
2027 onward	Applicable transitional deadlines and requirements for certain regulated-product AI systems

Key Takeaway

The EU AI Act does not have a single effective date for every requirement. It uses a phased implementation approach, with obligations depending on the AI system, the organization’s role, and the applicable legal provisions.

Organizations should identify which requirements apply to their AI systems, document their compliance responsibilities, and monitor official EU guidance and legislative updates.
