Essential AI Concepts

Simple Explanation & Summary

This lesson explains the most important AI terms used in modern business.

The goal is not to become an AI engineer. Instead, the goal is to understand the terminology used when people discuss:

* AI solutions
* AI vendors
* AI tools
* AI projects
* AI automation
* AI agents
* Business AI strategy

Understanding these concepts makes it much easier to participate in AI-related discussions and make informed business decisions.

⸻

1. Large Language Models (LLMs)

What are LLMs?

Large Language Models (LLMs) are AI models designed to understand and generate human-like language.

Examples include:

* ChatGPT
* Claude
* Gemini
* Copilot

LLMs can process large amounts of text and generate useful responses based on patterns learned during training.

What can they do?

LLMs can:

* Answer questions
* Write reports
* Summarize documents
* Generate emails
* Analyze information
* Write code
* Explain concepts
* Brainstorm ideas

Business Example

Instead of spending two hours writing a project update, an employee can ask an LLM to create a first draft in seconds.

The employee can then review, correct, and improve the result.

Key Point

Most businesses use LLMs rather than building their own LLMs.

Creating a state-of-the-art LLM requires enormous amounts of computing power, data, expertise, and financial investment.

⸻

2. Machine Learning & Neural Networks

Machine Learning

Machine Learning (ML) is a method that allows computers to learn patterns from data.

Instead of explicitly programming every rule, a machine-learning system can learn patterns from examples.

Simple Definition

Machine Learning = how AI learns patterns from data.

⸻

Neural Networks

A neural network is a type of machine-learning model inspired loosely by the structure of biological brains.

Modern AI systems rely heavily on neural networks.

Business Takeaway

You generally do not need to understand the mathematics behind neural networks to use AI effectively.

The important distinction is:

Machine Learning = learning patterns from data.

Neural Networks = a type of machine-learning model.

4. AGI — Artificial General Intelligence

What is AGI?

Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) generally refers to a hypothetical AI system with broad, human-like intellectual capabilities across many different tasks.

Popular fictional examples include:

* Jarvis from Iron Man
* Skynet from Terminator

These are fictional examples, not real AGI systems.

Does AGI Exist Today?

There is no universally agreed-upon demonstration of AGI today.

Current AI systems are extremely capable, but they should not automatically be assumed to have human-like general intelligence or understanding.

Key Point

Today’s generative AI should not automatically be equated with AGI.

5. Narrow AI

What is Narrow AI?

Narrow AI is AI designed to perform a specific task or limited set of tasks.

Examples include:

* Spam filters
* Fraud detection
* Recommendation engines
* Face recognition
* Search ranking systems
* Predictive maintenance

Why Is It Important?

You have probably been using AI for years without realizing it.

Many AI systems used in businesses before the rise of ChatGPT were examples of narrow AI.

Key Point

Most traditional business AI has been narrow AI focused on specific problems.

8. Prompt Engineering

What is Prompt Engineering?

Prompt engineering is the practice of writing effective instructions for AI systems.

Weak Prompt

Write a marketing email.

Better Prompt

Act as a luxury-brand copywriter. Write a marketing email for professionals aged 35–50 promoting our premium watches. Use a sophisticated tone and include a clear call to action.

The second prompt provides more useful instructions.

Why Does It Matter?

Better instructions can produce more useful and predictable results.

Employees who know how to communicate effectively with AI can often get substantially better outputs than employees who simply ask vague questions.

Key Point

Prompt engineering = communicating effectively with AI.

⸻

9. Context Engineering

What is Context Engineering?

Context engineering is the process of providing an AI system with the information it needs to perform a task effectively.

Context can include:

* Company information
* Project details
* Business goals
* Team structure
* Policies
* Supporting documents
* Customer information
* Previous decisions

Example

Without Context

Create a project plan.

The AI has very little information about the project.

With Context

Create a project plan for our IAM implementation supporting 500 employees using Microsoft Entra ID. Include project phases, dependencies, risks, stakeholders, and a proposed timeline.

The second request gives the AI much more useful information.

Key Point

Good context can be just as important as a good prompt—and often more important.

10. Context Window

What is a Context Window?

A context window is the amount of information an AI model can process within a particular interaction.

A simple analogy is:

Context window = AI’s working memory for the current interaction.

Different AI models have different context limits.

What Happens When Context Becomes Too Large?

When relevant information is missing, truncated, or otherwise outside what the model can effectively use, responses may become less consistent.

Possible problems include:

* Missing important details
* Inconsistent answers
* Repetition
* Incorrect assumptions
* Hallucinations

Best Practice

For long-running projects, it can be useful to periodically create a concise summary containing:

* Important decisions
* Current status
* Requirements
* Relevant facts
* Next steps

Then use that summary as context for a new conversation when appropriate.

Key Point

Do not assume an AI conversation can function as unlimited memory.

11. AI Agents

AI agents are one of the most important concepts in modern AI.

What is an AI Agent?

An AI agent is an AI system designed to perform tasks by taking actions, often across multiple steps, with varying degrees of autonomy.

Traditional AI Interaction

You ask:

“Write a report.”

The AI writes a report.

AI Agent

You ask:

“Create a sales report from this data.”

An agent might:

1. Retrieve the required data
2. Analyze the information
3. Identify important trends
4. Create charts
5. Generate the report
6. Revise the report based on feedback

Simple Difference

Chatbot / Generative AI → primarily generates responses or content.

AI Agent → can plan and perform actions to accomplish a task.

The exact capabilities of an agent depend on the tools and permissions it has.

13. AI Harnesses

What Is an AI Harness?

An AI harness can be thought of as an environment that gives an AI system access to tools, files, context, and capabilities needed to perform more complex work.

Examples of AI development or work environments include:

* Claude Code
* Codex

Depending on the environment, AI may be able to work with:

* Files
* Folders
* Documents
* Code
* Data
* Applications
* Development tools

Business Benefits

AI can potentially:

* Create PowerPoint presentations
* Build spreadsheets
* Analyze CSV files
* Generate reports
* Modify documents
* Build applications
* Work across multiple files

Key Point

An AI harness gives AI a working environment rather than limiting it to a simple chat box.

14. Harness Engineering

What is Harness Engineering?

Harness engineering is the process of designing the environment around an AI system so that it can perform useful work reliably.

This can involve:

* Skills
* Tools
* MCP connections
* Memory
* Context
* Permissions
* Business rules
* Evaluation systems

The goal is to give AI the right capabilities and information while controlling what it is allowed to do.

⸻

15. Skills

What Are AI Skills?

AI skills are reusable instructions or workflows designed to help an AI perform a particular type of task consistently.

Think of them as:

Templates or reusable expertise for AI.

Example

A security consultant creates a:

Risk Assessment Skill

The skill could contain instructions for:

* Identifying risks
* Classifying risks
* Evaluating impact
* Recommending controls
* Formatting the final assessment

Other employees can reuse the same skill.

Benefit

Skills allow organizations to turn individual expertise into reusable AI workflows.

Key Point

Skills make expert knowledge more reusable and consistent.

16. MCP — Model Context Protocol

What is MCP?

Model Context Protocol (MCP) is a protocol designed to allow AI systems to connect with external tools and data sources in a standardized way.

This can allow AI applications or agents to interact with systems such as:

* Salesforce
* Jira
* GitHub
* Asana
* Google Drive

Example

Instead of manually opening a CRM and searching for incomplete customer records, you could potentially instruct an AI system:

“Find customer records missing contact information.”

If the AI has the appropriate MCP connection and permissions, it can use the connected system to retrieve or act on the required information.

Key Point

MCP can provide a standardized way for AI applications to interact with external tools and business systems.

The AI still needs appropriate authentication, permissions, and safeguards.

17. Memory

What is AI Memory?

AI memory refers to information that can be stored and reused across interactions.

Examples might include:

* Company structure
* Project background
* Business processes
* User preferences
* Organizational standards
* Important decisions

Benefit

Instead of repeatedly explaining the same information, an AI system can potentially use previously stored knowledge.

Example

Without memory:

“Our company has 500 employees, uses Microsoft 365, and follows this security framework…”

With appropriate memory:

The system may already have access to this information.

Key Point

Memory allows relevant information to persist beyond a single interaction.

Memory implementations vary significantly between AI products.

18. RAG — Retrieval-Augmented Generation

What is RAG?

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) combines an AI model with external knowledge sources.

Instead of relying only on information learned during model training, the system retrieves relevant information from external documents or databases and provides it to the model.

Simple Model

User Question
      ↓
Search Company Knowledge
      ↓
Retrieve Relevant Documents
      ↓
Give Documents to AI
      ↓
Generate Answer

Example

Instead of answering a question using only general knowledge, an internal company AI system could retrieve information from:

* Company policies
* SharePoint documents
* Internal procedures
* Knowledge bases
* Product documentation

Benefit

RAG can make AI responses more relevant to an organization’s current and proprietary information.

Key Point

RAG = AI + retrieved external knowledge.

⸻

19. Fine-Tuning

What is Fine-Tuning?

Fine-tuning involves additional training of a pre-trained AI model on a specialized dataset for a particular purpose.

For example, an organization might fine-tune a model to improve performance on a specific type of:

* Classification
* Writing style
* Domain task
* Structured output

Important Distinction

Fine-tuning is not the same as RAG.

RAG gives the model relevant information at the time of the request.

Fine-tuning changes the model through additional training.

Business Takeaway

Fine-tuning is generally a more technical activity and is not something most ordinary business users need to perform themselves.

How AI Skills Are Changing

The value of employees is increasingly shifting from:

Creating everything manually

toward:

Directing, verifying, improving, and integrating AI-generated work.

This does not mean human expertise becomes unimportant.

Instead, human judgment becomes increasingly important for:

* Defining goals
* Providing context
* Evaluating quality
* Making decisions
* Managing risks
* Handling exceptions
* Taking responsibility for outcomes

Final Message

The core lesson is:

AI success is not just about having access to ChatGPT, Claude, or another AI tool.

Real business value comes from combining:

* The right AI model
* Good prompts
* High-quality context
* Business knowledge
* Useful tools
* Reliable workflows
* AI agents
* Automation
* Human oversight

The progression can be summarized as:

Ask AI
  ↓
Give AI better instructions
  ↓
Give AI better context
  ↓
Connect AI to business knowledge
  ↓
Connect AI to business tools
  ↓
Let AI perform multi-step tasks
  ↓
Automate real business processes

Professionals who learn to direct, evaluate, and integrate AI effectively can gain a significant productivity advantage.

The future of AI-enabled work is therefore not simply:

“AI does the work.”

It is increasingly:

“Humans define the goals, provide the context, supervise the AI, and use AI systems to accomplish more.”
